# Golden traces from the emulator

A golden trace is one real battle that was played in DeSmuME against the US Platinum ROM
and recorded turn by turn from battle RAM. `tests/test_golden.py` replays each recorded
turn through `step`, using the recorded actions and the realized chance outcome. The
engine's result must then equal the next recorded turn on every field in
`tools.trace_to_state.COMPARED_FIELDS` (spec §7, "Golden traces").

The pipeline has three stages:

1. `tools/trace_battle.lua` runs inside DeSmuME and writes a **raw trace**. This is a
   JSON-lines file with one `turn` record at every turn boundary and one `hit` or
   `move_end` record for each move-execution event in between.
2. `uv run python -m tools.trace_to_state --trace <raw.jsonl> --out tests/golden/roark_01.json --party tests/fixtures/golden_party_roark_01.toml`
   bundles the raw records with the player's party and the trainer id. It then converts
   the bundle once, to check that it converts, and writes it out. The bundle holds
   extracted battle state only and never ROM bytes. It is the only trace file that is
   committed. Raw traces belong in `traces/`, which is git-ignored. For Kaizo, pass
   `--game kaizo --save <save.dsv>` instead of `--party`: the party is read from the
   save (`SaveMon.to_spec`), and the trainer and tables come from the Kaizo data set,
   which the bundle records as `"game"` (`docs/tracing_kaizo.md`). The bundle format is
   `nuzline-golden-trace/2`: each party entry also carries `exp`, `exp_boost`, `pokerus`
   and `friend_bonus` (`PokemonSpec`; `exp_boost` is resolved with the save's trainer,
   the OT test of `BattleSystem_PokemonIsOT`). A `/1` bundle still loads, with the
   `PokemonSpec` defaults for those keys (the base exp of the level, boost 100, 0, 0).
3. `trace_to_states(bundle, tables, names)` rebuilds the turn-0 state from the party and
   the trainer, overlays each recorded turn, and infers both actions and the `Outcome` of
   every turn. The golden test replays those turns.

The ROM, saves, save states and raw traces are never committed (`.gitignore`: `*.nds`,
`*.sav`, `*.dsv`, `*.ds[0-9]`, `traces/`).

## The recorded battle: Roark, `roark_01`

- **Opponent:** Roark, the Oreburgh Gym Leader. The trainer is resolved the way
  `nuzline --trainer roark` resolves it: the first trainer named Roark whose class starts
  with "leader" (trainer 246 in the current tables). The converter builds his party with
  `build_trainer_party` and his bag with `Trainer.items`, which holds two Potions.
- **Terrain:** the trace does not record the battle terrain. The converter uses
  `Trainer.terrain` (Roark: `TERRAIN_BUILDING`, tools/terrain.py). For a trainer whose maps
  give several terrains, pass the one the battle started on with
  `python -m tools.trace_to_state ... --terrain GRASS`; it is stored as the bundle's
  `terrain` and must be one of `Trainer.terrains`.
- **Player:** `tests/fixtures/golden_party_roark_01.toml`. It starts as a copy of the
  M1a sample party (`tests/fixtures/sample_party.toml`), in this order:

  | Slot | Pokemon | Level | Moves | Item | Ability |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | Monferno | 14 | Scratch, Leer, Ember, Mach Punch | none | Blaze |
  | 1 | Prinplup | 14 | Pound, Growl, Bubble, Water Sport | none | Torrent |
  | 2 | Grotle | 14 | Tackle, Withdraw, Absorb, Razor Leaf | Sitrus Berry | Overgrow |
  | 3 | Starly | 10 | Tackle, Growl, Quick Attack, Wing Attack | Oran Berry | Keen Eye |
  | 4 | Shinx | 13 | Tackle, Leer, Spark | none | Rivalry |
  | 5 | Budew | 10 | Absorb, Growth, Water Sport, Stun Spore | none | Natural Cure |

  Every Pokemon has a Hardy nature, IVs 20, EVs 0, is male and starts at full HP.

  A real save will not hold these exact Pokemon, because IVs, natures and EVs come from
  the user's own playthrough. Before recording, **edit the TOML to match the save
  exactly**: species, level, nature, IVs, EVs, moves in slot order, held item, ability
  and gender. The IVs and EVs can be read from the save with any Gen 4 save editor. The
  converter compares the TOML-built turn-0 state with the first recorded turn on
  species, level, max HP, moves and item, and raises `TraceMismatch` naming the first
  difference it finds. A wrong IV or EV usually shows up there as a max-HP difference.
  Other stat errors only show up later, as damage that fits no roll.
- **Fight rules for a usable trace:**
  - Lead with slot 0. Whether you win or lose does not matter: the recording ends where
    the battle ends, and the converter replays every turn up to there
    (`trace_to_state`, which stops only at `state.over`).
  - A player Pokemon may faint: the engine treats a player faint as a death, not the end
    of the battle (`_faint_check`, `needs_replacement`), and the converter replays the
    player's replacement into the slot the next record shows (`apply_turn`). Two
    replacements on one turn are refused (`TraceError`): a double KO, or a newcomer that
    faints on entry (the record shows only the last active).
  - Use only moves and switches, never a bag item: the player's bag is not modelled.
    Roark's own Potion use is inferred and replayed (actions 10 to 13).
  - The golden test needs at least three turns: three turn boundaries plus the final
    `turn` record.

## Running DeSmuME with the script

1. Use DeSmuME 0.9.13 or later for Windows (x64). Its Lua runs on Lua 5.1 with the
   `bit` library, which the script needs. Install `lua51.dll` next to `DeSmuME.exe` if
   the Lua window reports that it is missing.
2. Load the US Platinum ROM (`CPUE`) and the prepared save. Walk to Roark. Make a save
   state just before talking to him, so that a failed take can be retried.
3. Edit the config block at the top of `tools/trace_battle.lua`:
   - `TRACE_PATH`: where the raw trace goes, for example
     `<project folder>/traces/roark_01.jsonl`. DeSmuME's Lua window takes no arguments, so this
     global is set in the file.
   - `BATTLE_SYSTEM`: leave it `nil` on the first run. The script then finds the battle
     structures by itself once a battle starts ("Locating the battle structures").
4. Open Tools > Lua Scripting > New Lua Script Window, browse to `tools/trace_battle.lua`
   and click Run. Then talk to Roark. The Lua console prints `trace: located ...` when
   the battle starts and one `trace: turn N` line per turn boundary.
5. Play the fight. When the battle ends, the script writes a final `turn` record (the
   result screen) and prints `trace: battle over`. Click Stop.
6. Convert the trace and run the test:

   ```bash
   uv run python -m tools.trace_to_state --trace traces/roark_01.jsonl \
       --out tests/golden/roark_01.json --party tests/fixtures/golden_party_roark_01.toml
   uv run pytest tests/test_golden.py -v
   ```

   The converter prints one line per turn: the inferred actions, the rolls that fit
   each hit, and any fields on which the engine's best replay disagrees with the trace.
   A golden failure means that no replayed candidate matches the trace. Usually that is
   an engine discrepancy: fix it as the decomp says, and log it in
   `docs/discrepancies.md`. It can also be a chance field that the converter did not
   enumerate on that turn (see the inference rules below), so check that first.

## RAM layout

All offsets come from the decomp's struct definitions (`external/pokeplatinum`, pinned in
`docs/decomp_paths.md`). They were laid out by hand with the ARM EABI rules that the
DS compiler follows: natural alignment, 4-byte pointers and ints, and bitfields packed
into their declared unit. The decomp names some padding members by their offset, such
as `padding0060`, `padding310A`, `unread_2440` and `padding0026_06`. Every chain of
offsets below is pinned to one of those named offsets, so an error in one chain cannot
move another. No ROM-built `.sym`/`.xMAP` was available when these were derived. A
build of the decomp (`make`, which yields `build/pokeplatinum.us.xMAP`) does not help
here either, because the battle structures are heap-allocated: only their offsets are
static.

### Static check against the ROM code

`uv run --group rom python -m tools.rom.offsets [--game kaizo]` (`tools/rom/offsets.py`,
tested by `tests/tools/rom/test_offsets.py`) reads the script's `SYS`, `CTX` and `MON`
tables and, for each field, disassembles decomp functions that access it (the list in
`ACCESSES` cites the source line of each access). It follows the struct pointer through
each function and pins each listed access to one instruction, using its width, whether it
is a load or a store, the constant stored, the register used and its position. A field
passes only when every pinned instruction uses exactly the script's offset, so the
script giving a field its neighbour's offset fails (the tests try every such swap). `MON`
fields are read through `battleMons[battler]`, and `MON.size` is the multiplier applied
to the battler index. Kaizo's overlay16 code moved by +4 to +0x20 bytes, so each function
is read at its shifted address, and its instruction sequence must match vanilla's.

All fields pass on both ROMs, with one correction: `seedRandNext` is at `0x2448`, not
`0x2444` (`BattleSystem_RandNext` loads it with the literal `0x2448`). The decomp's
`unread_2440` name is 4 bytes off: `BattleSystem_SetUnread_2440` stores a byte at
`0x2444`, `BattleSystem_GetNetworkID` reads `0x2446`, and `seedDTO` is at `0x244C`. The
converter does not read `rng` yet, so earlier synthetic tests are unaffected.

The rows below give the hand derivation. The behaviour at run time was checked on the

`command` values come from `enum BattleControlSequence`
(`include/battle/battle_controller_player.h`). The script uses these, and each is
checked against the enum's `UNUSED_nn` markers (`tests/test_trace_to_state.py` also
checks the script's `CMD` table against the enum):

- `INIT_COMMAND_SELECTION = 4`: the turn boundary. `COMMAND_SELECTION_INPUT = 5` also
  counts when no record has been written for the current `totalTurns` yet, for a run
  that located the structures after `INIT` had already passed.
- `UPDATE_HP = 27`: a move's damage is about to be applied. `damage` holds minus the
  uncapped damage (`BattleControllerPlayer_UpdateHP`,
  `battle_controller_player.c:3341`).
- `EXEC_SCRIPT = 21` with `commandNext == LOOP_FAINTED = 33` and
  `attacker == defender`: a confusion self-hit. It never enters `UPDATE_HP`: the status
  check (`battle_controller_player.c:2628-2638`) sets `defender = attacker`, stores
  minus the damage in `hpCalcTemp`, and runs `subscript_hurt_self_in_confusion.s`,
  whose HP update is a script subroutine, before `LOOP_FAINTED`. The script writes a
  `hit` record with `damage = -hpCalcTemp` and `defender = attacker`. (A disobedient
  mon hurting itself, `:3168`, would match too; the player's own Pokemon always obey
  in Roark's fight.)
- `MOVE_END = 39` and `RESULT = 41`: the end of a move, and the battle result.

`BattleContext_Main` (`battle_controller_player.c:194`) runs one command per call. The
script polls `command` once per frame, so it relies on one call per frame: every command
it watches must be visible for at least one frame boundary. Verified 2026-11-08 (Kaizo
Roark trace, `traces/kaizo_roark_01.jsonl`): all 40 turn boundaries (turns 0 to 39) were
seen, and the recording holds 48 `hit` and 48 `move_end` records, so no watched command
was missed.

### `Party` (`include/party.h`) and `Pokemon` (`include/struct_defs/pokemon.h`)

`Party`: `capacity` `0x00`, `currentCount` `0x04`, `pokemon[6]` from `0x08` with stride
`0xEC`. A `Pokemon` is `BoxPokemon` (`0x88`) followed by `PartyPokemon`.

- `personality` `0x00`, flags `0x04` (bit 0 `partyDecrypted`, bit 1 `boxDecrypted`),
  `checksum` `0x06`, then four `0x20`-byte data blocks from `0x08`. The blocks are
  encrypted with `checksum` as the seed: each halfword is XORed with
  `LCRNG_NextFrom(&seed)`, which is `seed = seed * 1103515245 + 24691`, returning
  `seed >> 16` (`math_util.c:217`). Their order is `BoxPokemon_GetDataBlock`'s table,
  indexed by `(personality & 0x3E000) >> 13` (`pokemon.c:4861`).
- Block A: `species` `0x00`, `heldItem` `0x02`. Block B: `moves[4]` `0x00`,
  `moveCurrentPPs[4]` `0x08`.
- `PartyPokemon`, encrypted with `personality` as the seed: `status` `0x88`, `level`
  `0x8C`, `hp` `0x8E`, `maxHP` `0x90`.

The script reads the active battler of each side from its `BattleMon`, which holds the
live values. It reads the other party members from the decrypted `Party`.
`selectedPartySlot[battler]` indexes that `Party` (verified 2026-11-08, Kaizo Roark trace,
`traces/kaizo_roark_01.jsonl`: Roark's slots follow his party order Bonsly 438, Lileep 345,
Gible 443, Corsola 222, Shuckle 213, Cranidos 408; the battle reorders its display order
through `partyOrder`, not the `Party` itself).

### Locating the battle structures

`BattleSystem` and `BattleContext` live on the heap. With `BATTLE_SYSTEM = nil`, the
script scans main RAM (`0x02000000`-`0x023FFFFF`) on every frame for a `BattleMon` that
matches the player's lead. That is a word-aligned address where `species` equals
`LOCATE_SPECIES` (set in the config to the lead's species id, 391 for Monferno),
`level` equals `LOCATE_LEVEL`, and `curHP <= maxHP <= 999`. From such a match it takes
`BattleContext = match - 0x2D40`. It then scans for a word holding that address with
`BattleContext` at `+0x30`, which gives `BattleSystem`, and prints both. The scan stops
at the first match. It can take a second when the battle starts.

Once the scan has printed `BattleSystem`, put that address into `BATTLE_SYSTEM`. Later
runs from the same save state then skip the scan, because heap layout is deterministic
for a given save state.

structures exactly as `trace_battle.lua` does and, at every turn, write one line per check
below to `traces/probe.txt` with the address, the value and what it should be. It records
no trace. `docs/tracing_kaizo.md` is the step-by-step guide for Kaizo. By hand, each of
these can be checked in DeSmuME's RAM viewer (Tools > View Memory), starting from the

#### Why the probe logged nothing

`tools/probe_battle.lua` opened its file and wrote the header, then never logged the
"located" line or any check. It has the same locate code (a scan every 120 frames, the same
`LOCATE_SPECIES`/`LOCATE_LEVEL` match), the same `while true ... emu.frameadvance()` loop
and the same polling as `trace_battle.lua`, which did locate in the same battle, so a wrong
address or a bug in its own logic is unlikely, and no change was made. The most likely
causes are in how the two ran together: two Lua windows each running a long
blocking scan loop in one DeSmuME process (each scan reads a million words and repeats that
for every candidate, so two scans at once can stall the emulator), or the probe's window
stopping on an error that was not reported. The run did not keep the console output of the
probe window. To diagnose it, run the probe alone, in one window, and send what the Lua
console prints.

## Raw trace format

The raw trace is JSON lines. All ids are the decomp's own: species, move, item. Sides are
battlers: 0 is the player and 1 is the opponent.

```json
{"kind": "turn", "turn": 0, "command": 4, "rng": 123456789, "field": 0,
 "sides": [{"active": 0, "stages": [6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6],
            "party": [{"species": 391, "level": 14, "hp": 41, "max_hp": 41, "status": 0,
                       "moves": [10, 43, 52, 183], "pp": [35, 30, 25, 30], "item": 0}, ...]},
           {...}]}
{"kind": "hit", "turn": 0, "attacker": 0, "defender": 1, "move": 52, "damage": 7, "crit": 0,
 "active": [0, 0], "hp": [41, 30], "status": [0, 0], "stages": [[6,...], [6,...]]}
{"kind": "move_end", "turn": 0, "attacker": 1, "move": 33, "flags": 1}
```

- `turn` is written at each `INIT_COMMAND_SELECTION` (or `COMMAND_SELECTION_INPUT`,
  see above), and once more at `RESULT` (the last record; `"command": 41`). `stages` is the active battler's `statBoosts` in decomp
  order. `field` is `fieldConditionsMask`. `rng` is `seedRandNext`, which the converter
  does not use yet and keeps for a later RNG cross-check.
- `hit` is written at each `UPDATE_HP` with a non-zero `damage`, and at each confusion
  self-hit (`attacker == defender`, `damage` from `hpCalcTemp`; its `move` and `crit`
  are stale and the converter ignores them). `damage` is the positive
  uncapped damage, `crit` is `criticalMul > 1`, and `active`, `hp`, `status` and
  `stages` are both battlers' values at that moment (before the HP drop). `crit` may be
  `null` when `criticalMul` cannot be trusted; the converter then tries both.
- `move_end` is written at each `MOVE_END`. `flags` is `moveStatusFlags`, where bit 0,
  `MOVE_STATUS_MISSED`, marks a miss.

## What the converter infers, and how

For each turn, `tools/trace_to_state.py` works out the following:

- **Actions.** A move slot is the slot whose PP dropped. A switch is an active slot that
  changed while the old active mon is still alive, unless the foe used Roar / Whirlwind
  (a PP drop on one): then every legal action (moves, switches, Roark's usable bag slots)
  of a side without a PP drop is a candidate, whether its active slot changed or not. The
  drag's newcomer does not act, and a switch the drag undid can even end on the start slot
  (the switch-in dragged out, the start Pokemon dragged back). When the
  side did not act (it fainted first, flinched, or was fully paralyzed), every legal move
  slot and, for Roark, every usable bag slot is a candidate, and the replay below picks
  among them.
- **Hit, crit, roll.** Each `hit` record of a side sets `hit = 1` and `crit` for that
  hit index, and a `move_end` with `MISSED` sets `hit = 0`. The roll is matched against
  `damage_for_roll` (the engine's `damage_table`) on the **hit-time state**: the turn's
  start state with the record's `active`, `hp`, `status` and `stages` written over it.
  `power_mul` is 20 for Pursuit hitting a switching foe. When no roll fits, the
  converter raises `TraceMismatch`, which names the move, the damage and the engine's
  table. Rolls that give the same damage cannot be told apart by any trace, and every
  later field of the turn is the same whichever is used, so the converter records all
  of them (`Turn.roll_candidates`) and uses the lowest. A hit whose `crit` is `null`
  raises `AmbiguousTrace`, carrying every candidate, when both a crit and a non-crit
  roll fit: whether a crit happened is an observable game event that the outcome must
  get right.
- **Present.** A `hit` record of Present is its damage path: the converter tries
  `Outcome.present` 0, 1, 2 (power 40, 80, 120) and keeps the lowest power with a fitting
  roll. The heal path runs no `CalcDamage` and so writes no `hit` record: a Present that
  connected with no `hit` record is replayed with `present` 0 and the heal
  (`PRESENT_HEAL`), and the replay that matches the target's HP gain is kept.
- **Nature Power, Tri Attack.** Records name `moveCur`, so a Nature Power's `hit` and
  `move_end` records name the move it called (`order.called_move`: Tri Attack on Roark's
  BUILDING terrain) while its PP is Nature Power's; the converter matches them against the
  called move. Tri Attack's status (`Outcome.tri_attack` 0-2: burn, freeze, paralysis) is
  enumerated with its `secondary` whenever the move connected; the target's status in the
  next record picks it. Without a proc the value is never read and every value replays the
  same: the converter keeps 0, which then means nothing.
- **Roar.** `Outcome.roar_level` (only when the user's level is below its target's: the
  foe's active Pokemon or the one it switches in) and `Outcome.roar_target` (the rank among
  the target side's eligible bench, when it has two or more) are enumerated for a Roar that
  connected; the next record's `active` (and the dragged Pokemon's HP after Stealth Rock)
  picks them. A failure (no bench, or the level check) leaves the foe's active unchanged.
- **Protect.** A Protect / Detect user's `move_end` record carries `MOVE_STATUS_FAILED`
  (bit 6) when `BtlCmd_TryProtection` failed: the converter sets `Outcome.protect` to 0
  then, else 1 (a success; a chain of 0 or a user acting last ignores the roll). A move it
  blocked writes no `hit` record and its `move_end` has `MOVE_STATUS_PROTECTED` (bit 15).
- **Level-ups and learn-move prompts.** The engine pays exp and levels inside `step`
  (`engine/levelup.py`); `apply_turn` then runs the learnset scans and answers the turn's
  learn-move prompts (`Turn.learn_choices`) before the replacements, as the decomp's
  `TurnEnd` does (battle_controller_player.c:1846-1848). The per-turn fit replays no answer
  and leaves the moves and PP of a slot with a pending prompt uncompared; then, from the
  chosen candidate, the converter searches the answers depth first, prompt by prompt in the
  engine's order (player slot ascending), each over giving up and every slot whose move is
  not an HM (the game's only rule, `Item_IsHMMove`, src/item.c:306-315), not the search's
  masks. The first answers that match the next record win. A slot whose move a prompt
  replaced this turn shows the new move's PP; it counts as used when the side's `hit` or
  `move_end` records name its old move. Refused: an answer that replaces a move the AI has
  seen (the AI's move-id list keeps the old id, trainer_ai.c:2705-2716), another option
  `levelup.learn_options` masks, and any move the engine does not support learned by an
  answer or into an empty slot (ruling D10). A party file whose exp is not of its level is
  refused too.
- **Quick Claw.** `speedRand` is not dumped. For a side whose start-of-turn active Pokemon
  holds a Quick Claw, the converter replays both values of the start state's `qc_roll`
  (the draw that orders this turn: the battle start's on turn 0, else the previous turn's
  `Outcome.quick_claw`), the carried value first so that a tie keeps it. When the other
  value fits better, it is written back into the previous turn's `Outcome.quick_claw`
  and that turn is replayed again (its `mismatched` is recomputed: its end-of-turn order
  read the same draw). The turn's own `quick_claw` is also enumerated when the battler on
  the field at the turn's end holds the claw (the end-of-turn order reads it). The order
  shows only through the replay (a stat change or a faint before the other side's hit),
  so an order that changes no compared field leaves the carried value.
- **The other chance fields** are `speed_tie`, `end_speed_tie`, `full_para`, `thaw`,
  `infatuation`, `secondary`, `contact_proc` (only when the defender, the target's
  start-of-turn active or the Pokemon it switches in, has Static, Poison Point, Flame Body or
  Cute Charm) and `duration`. These are enumerated only where they can matter:
  - `speed_tie` when the start state's speeds are equal.
  - `end_speed_tie` when the speeds are equal in the start state, after the turn's
    switches, or after the switches with the next record's status and stages, or with the
    next record's active slots, status and stages (a switch-in, a Roar's drag, a
    paralysis or a Speed change can create the tie).
  - `full_para` and `thaw` when the side's start-of-turn active mon is paralyzed or
    frozen at the start, in any `hit` record of the turn, or in the next record. A
    status inflicted earlier in the turn (Stun Spore, Spark, Static) can stop the
    slower mon on the same turn.
  - `infatuation` when the side uses a move and is in love at the start (the engine's
    carried `infatuated`; the trace has no volatile bits) or its foe uses Attract. An
    immobilized mon spends no PP and writes no record, so the replay with 1 matches the
    unchanged PP and HP; the one with 0 needs the PP drop. A confusion self-hit is taken
    from a `hit` record whose
  attacker is also its defender, and its roll is matched against `confusion_damage`.
  Every candidate (actions times outcome fields) is replayed through `apply_turn`:
  `step`, then the replacement of a fainted active Pokemon into the next record's active
  slot, the player's before the opponent's (`ReplaceFainted`,
  battle_controller_player.c:1848, serves battler 0 first, :4078-4130). The candidate
  that matches the next record on the most `COMPARED_FIELDS` wins. Ties go to the first
  candidate. A best candidate that still disagrees is kept: the golden test then fails
  on that turn and names the field. `Turn.player_slot` (and `ReplayTurn.player_slot`)
  is the player's replacement slot, -1 for none.
- **Draws that show only later: the refit.** A secondary confusion (Confusion, Psybeam,
  Water Pulse: `SECONDARY_CONFUSE`) and its duration show only as later self-hits, and a
  Bind-type trap's duration only on the turn its residual stops. `duration` is enumerated
  for `SECONDARY_CONFUSE` as for `CONFUSE_FOE` (2-5: `subscript_confuse.s:34`, `Random 3,
  2`, is `rand % 4 + 2`, `BtlCmd_Random`, battle_script.c:3200-3207) and for a trap
  (3-6: `subscript_bind_start.s:10`, `Random 3, 3`). Each turn is fitted against the next
  record only, so the first tied candidate is kept. When a turn's best candidate still
  mismatches, or the turn has a self-hit record of a side that is not confused at its
  start, the converter rewinds: for each earlier turn `i` from the previous one back
  five turns (latest first), and each of turn `i`'s other candidates that match its next
  record on every compared field (in candidate order: `secondary` 0 before 1, durations
  ascending), it replays turn `i` with that candidate and infers the following turns
  again as usual (Quick Claw write-backs included). The first that explains every turn
  from `i` on is kept; if none does, the original fit stays and the turn's `mismatched`
  names the fields. Two fits that replay every record identically cannot be told apart
  (on `kaizo_roark_02`, a Water Pulse confusion on turn 16 with duration 5 or on turn 18
  with duration 3): the rule picks the latest turn, then the lowest value.
- **A fainted battler's status.** The tracer reads an active battler's status from the
  BattleMon (`tools/trace_battle.lua:182-183`). The decomp zeroes the status only in the
  party copy of a Pokemon with no HP (`BattleController_EmitUpdatePartyMon`,
  battle_controller.c:1481-1486), which is what the engine models; a battler that faints
  at the battle's end is never replaced, so its BattleMon keeps the bits. The converter
  sets `status` and `status_counter` to 0 for every Pokemon at 0 HP in a record.

`COMPARED_FIELDS` is `species`, `level`, `hp`, `max_hp`, `status`, `status_counter`,
`item`, `moves`, `pp`, `active`, `stages` and `field_mask` (the traced
`fieldConditionsMask` bit for bit). The dump does not fill the
other `BattleState` fields. Each turn takes them from the engine's own replay of the
previous turn, so they are carried forward and never checked:

- per slot: `form`, `stats` (only max HP is dumped), `types`, `ability`,
  `ability_slot`, `item_consumed`, `gender`, `friendship`, `ivs`
- volatiles: `confusion` (fitted from self-hits by the refit above), `leech_seed`,
  `substitute_hp`, `taunt`, `trapped`, `trap_move`, `flinched`, `choice_lock`, `last_move`, `protect_count`,
  `rollout_count`, `charge_turn`, `recharge`, `pursuit_target`, `water_sport`,
  `fake_out_turn`, `move_hit`
- side conditions: `spikes`, `toxic_spikes`, `stealth_rock`, `reflect`,
  `light_screen`, `safeguard`, `mist`, `lucky_chant`, `tailwind`, `wish_timer`,
  `wish_hp`, `future_timer`, `future_damage`, `trainer_items`, `trainer_item_count`
- field: `weather_turns`, `mud_sport`
- hidden draws: `qc_roll` (fitted, see Quick Claw above)
- experience: `exp`, `evs`, `exp_mask` and the learn fields (the trace has no exp;
  `level`, `max_hp`, `hp`, `moves` and `pp` check the level-ups and learns)
- bookkeeping: `turn` (the final record at `RESULT` comes before `totalTurns++`, so it
  would always differ by one), `over`, `winner`, `player_faints`, `ai_seen_moves`,
  `ai_ability_known`

Of these, `stealth_rock`, `trapped`/`trap_move` and `substitute_hp` are the most useful
to add next. They are in `sideConditionsMask`, `BattleMon.statusVolatile` and
`moveEffectsData` respectively, and their offsets are still to be laid out.

## Known limits

- A turn with two replacements for one side (a newcomer fainting on entry) or one for
  each side (a double KO) is refused, not replayed.
- The refit looks back five turns at most. A draw that shows more than five turns later
  stays mismatched.
- A side that used no PP and changed no compared field (fully paralyzed, or a
  Potion at full HP) is told apart only by `trainer_items`, which is not dumped. The
  converter tries move candidates before bag slots, so it reads such a turn as a move
  that did not happen.
- The player's bag items are not modelled. A trace that uses one fails to convert,
  because no action candidate fits.
- Multi-hit moves are read as one `hit` record per hit. They have not been exercised on
  a real trace.
- The runtime checks (`selectedPartySlot` indexing the battle's `Party`, every watched
  command visible at a frame boundary, and the rest of "Verifying the addresses") were made
  on a Kaizo recording only. The vanilla battle structures are covered by the static check.
